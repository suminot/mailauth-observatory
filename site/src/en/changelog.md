# Change log

Changes to how the measurement is made, or to how it is aggregated,
are recorded here. When a figure moves against the previous month,
**this is what tells you whether the world changed or the measurement did.**

This list is generated by `mailauth changelog` (2026-11-07T22:47:05+00:00).
Version changes are detected by machine, but **what they did to the
figures has to be written by a person.** A month whose “interpretation” line is blank is one nobody has written up yet.

## 2026-11

### Changes to the method this month (written by hand)

- The outbound front-end check now includes the names that Proofpoint, Mimecast, Barracuda, Cisco, Trend Micro and others ask customers to publish in SPF for sending (only names found in each vendor's documentation). The number of US companies with an outbound front-end rose largely because of this.
- Rules were added for DKIM CNAME targets (SendGrid, HubSpot, Constant Contact, Salesforce and others) and SPF includes (KnowBe4, Mailgun, Oracle and Japanese delivery services). The rise in sending-service counts comes from this.
- Inferences without a layer were split into "services used for sending" and "DMARC report aggregation services". Domain-ownership TXT records alone are no longer counted as evidence of sending.
- Whether DMARC report destinations publish the record accepting the reports is now counted.
- MX hosts confirmed in operators' documentation (iCloud+, Seznam, Daum and others) were added to the platform check.
- The SPF terms that make DNS lookups are now counted through the include and redirect targets. Domains over the limit of 10, and domains whose targets have no SPF, were added to the summary.
- Counts by the final SPF `all` term (-all, ~all, ?all, +all, none) were added to the summary. Records that hand over with `redirect=` are counted by the `all` of the target.
- Domains that publish DMARC with an invalid record (two records, or p= missing or invalid) are now counted, as part of those publishing DMARC.
- Domains where DKIM was found are now counted by the shortest RSA key found (2048 bits or more, 1024, under 1024). Ed25519 keys are no longer given an estimated length.
- Counts were added for domains that publish DMARC without an aggregate report destination (rua), those among them with p=none, and BIMI records that give a certificate location.
- For Japanese companies, the URL given as the method of public notice in the annual securities report was added as a source of the official site (used only when the page carries the trade name). The rise in Japanese companies measured comes from this.
- Other domains linked from the official site's top page, where that page's title carries the trade name and the domain has MX, were added as candidate domains. The rise in the number of domains measured comes from this.
- The company's own domain that Japanese companies give as their method of public notice in the annual securities report was added as a candidate domain (domains of providers that post notices for others are excluded).
- For Japanese companies, the company's own domain given in the annual securities report now takes precedence as the official site. Some companies had a page that is not their own (such as a company-profile site) recorded as their official site.
- For US companies, the site the company states in its annual report now takes precedence (some still had the site from before a name change). The earlier site is kept as a candidate domain.
- Counts were added for domains where an SPF include or redirect target's domain is not registered, where the apex TXT response is estimated to exceed 1,232 bytes, where the DKIM keys found are Ed25519 only, and where DMARC carries tags removed in RFC 9989.
- A count was added for domains whose apex SPF record lets through more than 65,536 IPv4 addresses (include targets are not counted).
- {'Among sending domains, counts were added for the DNS-visible part of bulk-sender requirements (Gmail, Yahoo, Microsoft)': 'SPF, DKIM and DMARC.'}
- Counts were added for domains whose DMARC carries the tags introduced in RFC 9989 (np=, t=y).
- The difference from the previous month now includes DMARC policy changes for the same domains observed in both months, and counts of rua added or removed. A change from quarantine with pct= under 100 to none was not counted as a step back; this was fixed.
- Counts were added for domains whose DMARC rua points to a name reserved for examples (example.com and the like), and for domains with p=reject and a rua destination.
- _domainkey.<domain> is now queried; among domains where DKIM was not found under the selectors tried, those with names under it (likely keys under other selectors) are now counted.
- Per company, stages of how far a reachable MX, SPF senders and a DKIM key line up were added, along with the number of companies receiving mail on a domain other than their website's.
- Per domain, counts crossing the grounds for tying it to the company (stated by the company, certificate, the company's DNS, linked from the website) with the stage of mail-use evidence were added.
- For companies whose website domain has no MX, the same name under another TLD was added to the candidate domains (only where it has MX and its top page title carries the trade name).
- A table placing the Japan-listed and US-listed populations for the same month side by side was added to the summary page (no ordering or scoring).
- The Nikkei 225 constituents (225 companies) are now counted separately from the Japan-listed measurement without measuring again. The list uses the securities codes in a Wikipedia article (CC BY-SA 4.0).
- The route that finds domains by the organization name in certificates now also covers companies with a website (using results filled in during the month). Counts of subdomains found, and of those whose registrable domain has DMARC, were added.
- Domestic subsidiaries of Japan-listed companies (affiliates named in the annual securities report with a website registered in gBizINFO) are now measured as a population separate from the listed companies. They are not mixed into the listed-company figures.
- Counts were added for domains whose SPF uses ptr, and for domains whose DMARC gives a failure report destination (ruf); destinations are not shown.
- Subdomains with mail-like names (news., mail2. and the like) found in the certificate transparency records for the official site were added as subdomain candidates (only those with their own MX or _dmarc are measured). This takes effect from the next month in which CT is queried, and subdomain counts are not comparable with earlier months.
